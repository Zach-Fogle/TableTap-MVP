# TableTap Employee Walkthrough

Use this 15-minute session to test whether employees understand the workflow and
whether request ownership is clear. This is a usability walkthrough, not a live
restaurant pilot.

## Participants and setup

- One server
- One shift leader or support employee
- One facilitator and note taker
- Guest page open on a phone at `/table/7`
- Staff member signed in at `/pos`
- No real guest information or live service requests

Record the date, participant roles, application commit, device, and any help the
facilitator provides. Ask participants to think aloud without coaching them
toward a particular answer.

## Scenarios

### 1. Submit and claim a routine request

1. Submit a napkin request from Table 7.
2. Ask the staff participant to find it and explain what they would do next.
3. Ask them to claim it and move it to In Progress.

Observe whether the participant notices the table, request, owner, and status.

### 2. Reassign responsibility

Tell the participant that the current owner must help another table. Ask them to
transfer the request to the other participant and explain how the table's server
would stay informed.

### 3. Reconcile a duplicate

Submit the same request a second time and ask participants to decide whether it
is a duplicate, a second need, or an error. Record what information or controls
they expect before taking action.

### 4. Remove queue coverage

Tell participants that nobody can monitor the queue for ten minutes. Ask what
should happen to new requests and what guests should see. Record the preferred
fallback and who controls it.

### 5. Complete and verify delivery

Ask the owner to mark the original request Done. Ask both participants what Done
means and how they would confirm that the item physically reached the table.

## Closing questions

1. When would this queue help during a shift?
2. When would it add work or distract from service?
3. Who should monitor unassigned requests?
4. How should the table server learn that another employee completed a request?
5. Which request types belong here, and which should always remain verbal?
6. What would make you unwilling to use the system during a rush?

## Decision record

After the session, choose one outcome and explain the evidence:

- **Revise and repeat:** participants could not complete or explain the workflow.
- **Prepare a limited pilot proposal:** ownership and fallback were clear, and no
  critical technical or operational issue remained.
- **Stop:** the queue adds more coordination effort than the problem warrants.

Do not report a service improvement unless a separately authorized live
evaluation measures actual delivery time, unresolved requests, and staff effort.
